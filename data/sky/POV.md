# Point of view

Instant: **2026-10-13 02:00 UTC** (10 p.m. EDT, 12 October), a night during conference week.

Place: Morehead Star Theater, 35.913611° N, 79.050556° W.

Reduction, in `app/build_sky.py`, written to `view.json`:

1. Proper motion from epoch J2000 to the instant.
2. IAU 1976 precession to the equator of date.
3. Local sidereal time from the longitude, then altitude and azimuth.
4. Saemundsson refraction.

Nutation and aberration are not applied. They move a star by arcseconds.

## Dome

Latitude parked on Chapel Hill. Each star is a point in the local frame: east, north, and up, in parsecs from the parallax. The eye is at the theater. Drag looks around. Stars with no usable parallax sit at 800 pc; their direction is still the measured one.

Polaris comes out at altitude **36.105°**. The site latitude is 35.914°. Polaris is about 0.63° from the pole in 2026, so its altitude sits within that of the latitude. This night it is 0.19° above the latitude.

4,309 stars. All 37 Apollo guide stars are named, matched to the flight-computer vectors within 0.2° after precessing those vectors from 28 October 1968.

## Window

Latitude axis at **35° south**. Daily motion parked so Canopus is on the meridian (altitude 72.3°, azimuth 180°). The camera looks along that line with a 60° field, the training window. The stars are the same kind of points, in that horizon frame.

805 stars. Canopus and Regor fall inside this aim. Acrux is 45° from Canopus and Peacock is 68°, so they are above that horizon and outside this park. Driving the daily motion brings them into the window one at a time. No single 60° window holds Canopus and Peacock together.
