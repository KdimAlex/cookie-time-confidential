# Two frames for a sky mockup

The dome and the training window are different pictures. Draw both, or the mockup will show the wrong sky.

Coordinates for the labeled stars live in `apollo_nav_stars.json`. Theater geometry lives in `site.json`.

## Frame A — public dome

What a seat in the Star Theater sees when the projector is left at Chapel Hill.

- Site: 35°54'49" N, 79°03'02" W, 144 m. Dome 68 ft across, 44 ft floor to apex.
- Stars down to magnitude 6.5, on the order of 8,900, which is the Zeiss plate count. Do not plot a deeper survey.
- Horizon from the latitude axis parked at Chapel Hill, with the lens shutters cutting the ground.
- Projection: a hemisphere whose center is the instrument, near the center of the dome sphere.
- Epoch: the Apollo vectors are late-1960s places. A "tonight in October 2026" public sky needs precession from that frame to the date of the show. Say which epoch the picture uses.

## Frame B — training window

What an astronaut was drilled to recognize. A Morehead staffer told the group to ask to see the 27 guiding stars. That count is not in the published record. The computer list below is 37. McColman has also said the general course was 42. If the dome operator runs a set, label the picture with the count they actually used.

- Mask about 60 degrees across. Richard McColman (UNC) described the spacecraft view as nothing more than about a 60 degree patch. That figure is his summary, not a measured Gemini window drawing. Shop drawings are not in this folder.
- Label only the 37 guide stars, plus Sun, Earth, and Moon when the lesson calls for them. The other plate stars stay dim context.
- Move the latitude axis off Chapel Hill so southern guide stars can enter the window: Canopus, Acrux, Peacock, Achernar, Atria. The Zeiss latitude axis is what made a southern sky possible in a North Carolina dome.
- The projector lamp goes off, the operator shifts the sky, the lamp comes back on. That is the "spin-the-world" drill (RocketSTEM). The mockup can show it as a second attitude of the same window, not as a different catalog.

Named pairs worth placing in the window, from published accounts:

- Apollo 13, debris cleared: Altair and Vega.
- Apollo 13, into the Moon's shadow: Antares and Nunki.
- Faith 7 and Apollo 12 used star sightings too. The writeups do not name the pair. Leave those windows unlabeled rather than inventing stars.

## What not to mix

Frame A is the gift John Motley Morehead bought for the campus. Frame B is the lesson Tony Jenzano's staff built on top of it. The same dumbbell throws both pictures. The plywood trainer is what crops Frame A down to Frame B.
